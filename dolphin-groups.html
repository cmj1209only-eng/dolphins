Dolphin Groups & Population Structure

Exploring pod-size distributions (2021–2026) and Sarasota Bay resident life-history benchmarks

Sightings vs. Unique Individual Dolphins

A crucial distinction in marine mammal science separates sighting events from unique individuals:

  • 2,206 visual sightings (2021–2026) represent distinct encounter events—the same resident dolphin in Sarasota Bay can be photographed dozens of times across seasons.
  • Within those 2,206 sightings, 259 records explicitly counted total group size (individualCount), summing to 1,749 individual dolphin observations, with a median pod of 4 dolphins and a mean of 6.75 dolphins (ranging from solitary foragers up to 44 dolphins). The remaining 1,947 records verified presence without recording a full headcount and are left as NA rather than assumed to be 1.
  • Long-term dorsal-fin photo-identification by Wells et al. (2025) tracks 482 uniquely cataloged resident Sarasota Bay dolphins over five decades (~160–170 core residents alive in any single year).

Sarasota Bay Resident Life-History Benchmarks (Wells et al., 2025)

Because opportunistic GBIF sightings rarely distinguish calves from adults consistently, peer-reviewed demographic parameters from 482 known Sarasota Bay residents (Wells et al., 2025) provide essential biological context for interpreting pod structure:

Demographic Parameter Sarasota Bay Resident Value Biological Meaning for Pod Structure
Maximum Observed Lifespan 67 yrs (Females) / 52 yrs (Males) Multi-generational maternal bands persist for decades
Age at First Reproduction 9.6 yrs (Females; maturation at 8.5 yrs) Young females remain near natal home ranges
Peak Calving Window 81% of births in May–July Drives warm-season mother–calf nursery pods
Mean Calving Interval 3.5 years (up to 12 calves/female) Calves stay closely bonded with mothers for 3–6 years
First-Year Recruitment Rate 0.050 (annual birth rate 0.071) Slow life history makes calf protection critical
Annual Immigration Rate 0.003 – 0.013 Nearly closed resident community inside Sarasota Bay
