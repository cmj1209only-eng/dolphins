🐬 Florida & Sarasota Bay Bottlenose Dolphins

Exploring 2,206 Wild Dolphin Sightings Along Florida’s Coasts (2021–2026)

🔍 What This Graph Shows—and Why Sightings Rise and Fall

This interactive chart combines 2,206 verified wild bottlenose dolphin (Tursiops truncatus) sightings recorded along Florida’s coastlines from January 2021 through October 2026 (hover over any color bar or click a coastal zone in the legend to isolate it!). At first glance, you might think Florida’s dolphins migrate away every late summer—sightings soar to 343 in April during sunny dry-season weather, stay lively into May–July peak calving season (when over 81% of Sarasota Bay dolphin calves are born), and then plunge by more than half in August–October.

Here is the scientific twist: long-term dorsal-fin tracking shows that Sarasota Bay’s ~160–170 resident dolphins stay in their home bay all 12 months of the year! What actually changes across the calendar is human observer effort. Cool, calm winter and spring afternoons bring thousands of boaters, kayakers, and pier visitors out with cameras, whereas scorching late-summer heat, daily afternoon lightning storms, and major Gulf hurricanes push people indoors while dolphins keep hunting and whistling below the waves.
